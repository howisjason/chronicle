Where was it written down?

The 55 tales of Seán Ó Súilleabháin’s Folktales of Ireland (1966), each looked for in the manuscript it was taken from. The book’s notes date them 1930 to 1948. 52 name a volume of the Irish Folklore Commission’s manuscripts, and some of those volumes are now scanned on dúchas.ie. Where a link is given, it opens the manuscript item that starts on the page the book gives, in the collector’s hand. Of the 33 tales whose volume is online, 31 start exactly on the book’s page, sixty years later.

The book’s side is each tale’s note at the back of the book, giving a volume and pages in the Commission’s Main Manuscript Collection (now CBÉ, at University College Dublin), read from the archive.org scan. The manuscript side is dúchas.ie’s own catalogue: the item’s title, page label and date as dúchas gives them. A tale “lands” when an item there starts on the page the book gives. That is a match of pages, not of stories: no tale’s text was read, and some of the manuscripts’ own titles (“Scéilín”, a little story) say nothing about which tale they are. The county in grey is the book’s; dúchas mostly gives places for a whole volume, not a tale, and for 25 and 54 it gives only Kerry where the book says Cork.

Two Donegal tales, 16 and 53, are cited at pages 1312 and 2093 of volumes that run to 556 and 549 pages on dúchas. I don’t know why yet.

No tale’s text is copied here, only titles and references. The scans and their catalogue belong to the National Folklore Collection, UCD; this page only links to them.
