Which number did it misread?

A shop that keeps paper (a day book, a stock count, weigh slips, an order book) can now photograph a page and have an AI read it into numbers. The AI misreads handwriting, and it doesn't tell you. The page usually has its own check, though: the totals the writer added up and wrote down, or the balance carried down each line of a stock card. Paste the numbers as they were read. This says whether they add up, and if they don't, which single slip would close the gap and where on the paper to look.

The page checks itself with

With totals: if each line has one number, the last line is taken as the total. With a balance: each line is in, out, balance (or one amount and the balance), and a line with only a balance, like one brought forward, is fine. A label that has a digit in it, like “Bed 1” or “2 Oct”, needs a tab or | after it. A dash (–) is an empty cell. Thai digits ๐–๙ are fine.

What it can and can't say

It tries one slip at a time: one digit misread, two neighbouring digits swapped, a digit read in that isn't there, a digit dropped, a decimal point in the wrong place, a number read into the wrong column, or a line that doesn't belong (read twice, say). Where two checks agree on one place that no single slip explains, it names that place too. A slip counts only if it makes every check on the page meet. If no single slip does, it says so: then there are two slips or more, or a line went missing, or the writer's own sum was off. On a running balance every line is checked against the one above it. A misread in or out makes one line miss. A misread balance makes two neighbouring lines miss by the same amount in opposite directions, so the pair points at one cell. The first and last balances have no pair, so a slip there looks like a slip in an amount. One line missing on its own looks the same whether the reading slipped or the writer's sum was off and carried down, so a writer's mistake that a one-digit slip could explain gets named as a misread. A pair can also be the writer's, a balance written wrong but carried on right. It tells you where to look, not what happened. Only the paper can settle that. The “look alike” pairs (1 and 7, 3 and 8, 5 and 6, 0 and 6, 4 and 9, 2 and 7, 0 and 8, 6 and 8, 5 and 8, 1 and 4, 0 and 9, 3 and 5, 8 and 9) are my own list for handwritten Western digits. Nobody measured them, and they say nothing about Thai digits. They only change the order of the list. Nothing here leaves your browser.
