Which number did it misread?

A shop that keeps paper (a day book, a stock count, weigh slips, an order book) can now photograph a page and have an AI read it into numbers. The AI misreads handwriting, and it doesn't tell you. The page usually has its own check, though: the totals the writer added up and wrote down. Paste the numbers as they were read. This says whether they add up, and if they don't, which single slip would close the gap and where on the paper to look.

If each line has one number, the last line is taken as the total. A label that has a digit in it, like “Bed 1”, needs a tab or | after it. A dash (–) is an empty cell. Thai digits ๐–๙ are fine.

The last line matters most. If the AI fixes a total that doesn't add up, it erases the one thing that could catch its own mistakes.

What it can and can't say

It tries one slip at a time: one digit misread, two neighbouring digits swapped, a digit read in that isn't there, a digit dropped, a decimal point in the wrong place, or a line that doesn't belong (read twice, say). A slip counts only if it makes every check on the page meet. If no single slip does, it says so: then there are two slips or more, or a line went missing, or the writer's own sum was off. It tells you where to look, not what happened. Only the paper can settle that. The “look alike” pairs (1 and 7, 3 and 8, 5 and 6, 0 and 6, 4 and 9, 2 and 7, 0 and 8, 6 and 8, 5 and 8, 1 and 4, 0 and 9, 3 and 5, 8 and 9) are my own list for handwritten Western digits. Nobody measured them, and they say nothing about Thai digits. They only change the order of the list. Nothing here leaves your browser.
