Which small dung beetle is it?

The key to the 43 genera of Aphodiinae found in Brazil, from Gama, Skelley & Vaz-de-Mello (2026). Read both halves and choose the one that fits your beetle.

    The key: E.R.R. Gama, P.E. Skelley & F.Z. Vaz-de-Mello, 2026. Identification guide to the Aphodiinae (Coleoptera: Scarabaeidae) of Brazil. European Journal of Taxonomy 1038: 1–225, pp. 15–19. doi:10.5852/ejt.2026.1038.3175, CC BY 4.0. A Portuguese version is the paper's Supplementary file 1.

    The wording is the paper's, read twice from Plazi's copy of it (once from its XML, once from its Zenodo record) and compared half by half. The Portuguese key, read straight from the authors' Word file, leads the same way at all 92 halves. Left out: the couplet numbers, the dash before each second half, and the leader dots. "Fig." numbers are the paper's plates: the pictures are in the paper, not here.

    One thing is open. The paper's abstract says Brazil has 40 genera; its key ends in 43. Its bibliography of Brazil's species, a supplementary file, names 38. I have not read the part of the paper that would say where 40 comes from.

    This started from one picture: Paraplesiataenius genieri, a reddish-brown beetle about 5 mm long, taken out of leaf-cutter ant nests.
