Does a school’s yearbook add up?

The state gymnasium of St Mary Magdalene in Poznań printed a report every year. Five in a row, 1926/27 to 1930/31, are scanned and public. Wherever they say one number two ways (a row and its total, a count and its percentage, this year and last), the two are set side by side here.

33 checks: 29 meet, 4 miss. 23 were read on the printed page on both sides; 10 came through the scan’s OCR text on at least one.

Who was counted

Open. 1919/20’s classes add to 886, and the table prints 865; no swap of two neighbouring digits explains it. In 1926/27, 74 passed of 103 is 71.8%, and the report prints 82,5%, which 85 of 103 would give. In 1928/29, 49 of 97 is 50.5%, and it prints 62%, which 49 of 79 gives (a swap), and so would 60 of 97. Which number is wrong isn’t said here; the reports don’t say. In 1927/28 the doctor counted 510, the start-of-year number, while 514 passed through the school that year. In 1928/29 and 1929/30 he counted 3 more than the start (445 and 425); for 1928/29 the table’s total is 445 too, and for 1929/30 there is no table, and nothing I read says who the three were.

Roads. “print” means both sides were read on the page image of the scan; “ocr” means at least one came through archive.org’s OCR text by way of a page reader and was not seen on the page. Every page link opens the scan at that page.

Sources. Sprawozdanie Dyrekcji Państwowego Gimnazjum Św. Marji Magdaleny w Poznaniu, 1926/27, 1927/28, 1928/29, 1929/30, 1930/31, from the Jagiellonian Library (public domain), on archive.org. The ten-year table is on page 5 of the 1928/29 report.
