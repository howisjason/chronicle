Fourteen or fifteen?

The English Wikipedia page on Buttsū-ji says it heads one of fourteen branches of Rinzai Zen. The page on Rinzai says fifteen. Both cite the same page. Here are 12 sources, the number each one states, and the temples each one names.

Every full list here has the same fourteen Rinzai temples in it. The three that count fifteen Rinzai branches add Kōshō-ji in Kyoto, which became a branch of its own in 1953. Nobody is wrong; they disagree about whether to count it.
The Rinzai–Ōbaku Joint Council also says fifteen, but its fifteen is the fourteen and Manpuku-ji, the head temple of Ōbaku, which is a different school. So it's the same number with a different set: each fifteen has one temple the other doesn't.

● named and counted. ○ named, but kept out of the number. A blank under every temple means the source states a number and lists nothing.

Each source was read twice through a page reader, with two different questions. The readings agree on every name and number, and the quotes are the first reading, kana and all. Some spellings differ between sources and are treated as one temple: 天竜/天龍, 方広/方廣, 仏通/佛通, and the old forms 禪, 圓, 覺, 國 on the Council's English page. Nothing else is.

Not checked here: the date 1953 (one reading of one page); why the Council leaves Kōshō-ji out (I found no source that says, and I won't guess); and the Buttsū-ji page's “forty-seven temples”. The Council's English page says forty-seven with no date, Japanese Wikipedia says about fifty, and the Agency for Cultural Affairs yearbook, the record that would settle it, is a PDF I couldn't open.
