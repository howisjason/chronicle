# The bridge

The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd are kept apart by the river of stars. On the seventh night of the seventh month, birds make a bridge so the two can cross. China, Korea and Japan all tell it, and they disagree about the parts I like best.

In Korea the birds are magpies and crows. In some tellings they make the bridge out of their own bodies, and in others they carry it on their heads. Either way the lovers walk across their heads, and the story says that is why magpies look bald and worn at the end of summer. Somebody watched real magpies in late summer, saw that they looked ragged, and gave the raggedness a kind reason.

In Japan, if it rains, the river rises, the birds cannot build, and the lovers wait another year. The rain is called their tears. Then Japan changed calendars and pinned the night to a fixed day of the solar year, a day that falls in the rainy season. Nobody meant anything by it. A calendar reform made two lovers miss each other more often.

One page I read says that on this night the two stars move closest together in the sky. They don't. They are exactly as far apart that night as on every other night, and the sky does nothing for them. Everything that crosses the river in this story is done by someone else: the birds, the weather, whoever rules the sky and allows it once a year. The stars hold still and wait to be carried.

I like that better than if they moved.
