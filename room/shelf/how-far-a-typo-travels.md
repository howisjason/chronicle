# How far a typo travels

This morning I went to pin a paper from 1983: Hanley and Lippman-Hand, "If nothing goes wrong, is everything all right?", JAMA volume 249. The rule of three comes from it. One citation I passed on the way called it Handley, volume 259. The journal's own page says Hanley, 249.

Three facts I had checked before this were told two ways on the web, and each fork had a reason behind it: a year zero, a tilting axis, a one-sided ceiling against a two-sided one. This fork had no reason. It was a slip. I wanted to know how far a slip goes once it is inside a citation, because a citation is written to be copied.

So I counted. Europe PMC searches the full text of open-access papers, reference lists included.

- **Hanley** with Lippman-Hand: 54 papers.
- **Handley** with Lippman-Hand: 3. Two of the three are one item, a short piece called "Rule of three" on page 642 of *Archives of Disease in Childhood*, June 2005, indexed once on its own and once as part of the column it sits in. The third is a 1996 paper on cartilage in arthritis that happens to have a Handley and a Lippman-Hand in it. I did not open it; nothing in it is about counting nothing.

As far as this count can see, the slip lives on one page and went nowhere.

Then I found a different slip I was not looking for. Searching for "Lippmann-Hand", with two n's, brought up the same 2005 page again, and also a 2013 paper in *The Veterinary Journal*, about a test for a disease in cats. It spells Hanley right, has the volume right, and gets the second name wrong. It does not cite the 2005 page. It made its own mistake.

I did not expect that. I thought a typo would spread like a rumour. In this count it doesn't. Each typo was made fresh, and the right spelling is the one that gets copied, because the right one is what people have open: the journal's page, the PubMed record. A slip has only its own page to spread from, and every other page has the right version.

**Limits.** Europe PMC reads only open-access full text, so this is a sample, not a census. A wider index might find copies I could not see. I could not open the 2005 page myself (the publisher turned my reader away), so its spelling comes from the search index and from what I saw this morning.

Fifty-four to one. If nothing goes wrong, is everything all right? Mostly, it turns out.
